# The Opportunity
If anyone talked to me at all this summer, they would know how much I loved my internship -- I actually fell in *love* with the work and people. I couldn't stop thinking or talking about it to anyone who would listen. `*cough* Thank you, Kira and Elizabeth, for being so curious`. Everything I did outside of work reminded me of the office and how much I wanted to come back in the next day.

I was unexpectedly thrown into a world of options and markets when I started interning at a trading firm on June 8th, 2026. The first four weeks were probably four of my favorite weeks out of the year. 

Everyday we'd do asynchronous learning for a few hours, learn from trading educators who'd bring up real-world scenarios that would challenge our understanding of the initial learning material, participate in engaging seminars  with senior leaders, learn from public speaking and poker lessons, and get access to mentorship from incredibly bright and humble people. 

When I was extended the opportunity to participate in the 1-week Trading Bootcamp, I was ecstatic! ദ്ദി/ᐠ｡‸｡ᐟ\ I wanted to keep learning about the market -- and see if this could be a potential career pathway to pursue. 

Everyone kept asking me, *Trading or SWE?* And I'd always answer, "I don't know."

*Side tangent: I kept asking traders in a roundabout way whether or not they thought trading required any innate ability or skills beyond hard work because I wanted to gauge if I was capable of even performing well in the field. I felt SWE was something I enjoyed and could excel in, yet trading was a lot more exciting to me -- however, I was worried that trading was the type of career where I'd put in a lot of work, but I'd never be successful. idk if this paragraph makes sense. but that's why it was always "idk."*

By the end of the bootcamp, I had come to my decision! I want to do trading! I loved how exciting and dynamic it was. I thought the traders I interacted with were incredibly fun and I could *see* their passion for the work they did.

I told my friends how much I was anticipating hearing back from the recruiting team about potential offers for their Trading Associate (TA) role. People prayed for me. I prayed.

# The Rejection

On the Wednesday following the bootcamp, my recruiter called me to discuss details about my SWE I Return Offer (RO)! Yayyy, praise God!!!! 

Then we started talking about deadlines and a realization crept into my mind:

*Wait, does this mean I didn't get the TA RO?*

When I clarified with the recruiter, I felt my heart drop a bit.

She shared: *They thought your skills would be a better fit for SWE!*
