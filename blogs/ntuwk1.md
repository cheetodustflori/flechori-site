# Adios, Chicago

Back in March 2026, I knew I wanted to study abroad in Hong Kong or Taiwan to get expeerience living in a new country and to potentially improve my 中文程度 (Chinese proficiency). I eventually settled on National Taiwan University in 台北/Taipei, 台灣/Taiwan and after many, many trials (e.g. housing, visa, course requirements), I am grateful to say that I've started my program! 

After several embarrassingly tearful goodbyes, I started my journey to NTU on a Hello Kity Eva Air flight from O'Hare on August 27th, 2026. Thank you to my parents for coming to the airport with my luggage, drive me back home so I could pick up my letters, and honestly just being so patient with me haha.

And thank you to Nuri, Anna, and Kay for dropping me off :)

## Day 4 (8/31): Hospital
*Monday, August 31st*
- 4 AM: Woke up and decided that I was just going to show up at the hospital and see what happens
- 7:30 AM: Arrived at Taipei City Hospital Renai branch and grabbed a ticket for the physical exam; it was already semi-crowded with a lot of elderly people. Fortunately, I was the first in queue for the physical!
- 8 AM: I went into the physical exam office and there was a very, very kind nurse who helped me get set up. She spoke English to me, got my incorrect information verified with the secretary and walked me over several times to translate, and walked me through *allll* the tests I needed (dental, x-ray, blood, urine, blood pressure, vision). She saw I needed a dental check-up but I didn't have an appointment scheduled, so she had one of the female residents walk me over to the dental clinic to see if they could sneak in a *shorttt* appointment for me. The female resident was in her 20s and very kind and upbeat. She could the lead in a C-Drama. 
- 8:20 AM: ✅ Got my blood pressure and vision measured
- 8:45 AM: At the dental office, the secretary was initially frustrated with me because I didn't understand her instructions. She said all appointment slots were filled up for the day and I could end up waiting for the entire day without an appointment. Eventually, I said “我今天可以等” and showed her a Google Translate message that said, "I know you can't guarantee an appointment today, but I still appreciate you helping me out" 
- I think the initial frustration softened because subsequently they waived my x-ray examination since I was a foreigner and they didn't want me to pay the x-ray fee and then within the next 15 minutes they got me a consulation with one of the dental students! Praise the Lord!
- 9:15 AM: ✅ Got my teeth checked! The dental student was very sweet and she spoke English. I could hear an Australian accent and it turned out that she had studied in Australia!
- I also need to schedule a dental appointment when I get back to the states lol. My teeth need cleaning.
- 9:30 AM: ✅ Printed my proof of vaccination documents @ 7/11 and got them verified by the kind nurse
- 10 AM: Got my tests scheduled by the primary doctor -- he almost missed the urine test until I pointed it out so make sure your doctor double-checks tests when you sit with them! Overall the doctor was patient and kind as well.
- 10:20 AM: ✅ Paid for all my tests
- 10:30 AM: ✅ Got my first blood test and was... handed a tube for the urine sample
- 10:35 AM: ✅ Finished the urine test. This was probably the worst experience. I went into the public bathroom and initially entered one of the floor toilet stalls and it smelled like straight-up doo-doo. I could not breathe and I did not have a place to put my documents. Eventually I went into a different stall and it was much better.
- 10:45 AM: ✅ X-ray test!
![pic](/blog/taiwan/hosp1.jpg)
![pic](/blog/taiwan/hosp2.jpg)

I really admire all of these employees -- even the ones who are impatient or not as sweet -- because I know they come in to work each day to help people who are often very hard to help.
